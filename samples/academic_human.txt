The study examined the relationship between sleep duration and academic performance among 412 undergraduate students at three universities. Participants completed a questionnaire on sleep habits and provided their semester grades. Students who reported sleeping fewer than six hours per night had lower mean grades than those sleeping seven to eight hours (p < 0.01). However, the association weakened after controlling for course load and part-time employment. The authors note that self-reported sleep is imprecise, and that the sample was drawn from urban institutions only. Further work with wearable sensors would help clarify whether the effect persists.
