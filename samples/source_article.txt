The Great Barrier Reef is the world's largest coral reef system, stretching over 2,300 kilometres along the coast of Queensland. It is composed of thousands of individual reefs and hundreds of islands. Rising ocean temperatures cause corals to expel the algae living in their tissues, which turns them white in a process called bleaching. Scientists have recorded several mass bleaching events in the past decade, and recovery can take many years. Local tourism supports tens of thousands of jobs, so protecting the reef matters for the regional economy as well as for wildlife. Farmers upstream have been asked to reduce fertiliser runoff because excess nutrients encourage algae that smother young coral.
